# Supervised customer photo handoff

## Scope and existing contract

The technician selects photos on one job and hands the phone to the customer. The customer can view only that temporary selection and return through the existing authenticated `CustomerModeContainer` hand-back. This is an on-device handoff, not a remotely shareable link or a durable customer access policy.

The existing `JobMediaView` validates pages against actor, organization, job, session, policy, request generation and origin; it contains staff capture, tagging, archive and share controls. `MediaCompareView` already shows before/after without captions or staff details but is opened from the staff screen and does not itself provide protected hand-back. Reuse its presentation ideas, not its full staff-owned list or exit. Customer document presentation uses `JobEstimateProtectedOwner` and `JobCustomerDocumentsView` with protected hand-back and job scope. Neither customer presentation nor the canonical media asset schema has a customer visibility classification.

At server checkout `448c238a`, `GET /v1/jobs/:id/media` authorizes `job.view` on the job and excludes archived by default. `GET /v1/media/:id/download` authorizes `job.view` on the owning job and stored state, then returns a short-lived signed R2 URL (default 900 seconds); it does not itself reject archived assets. Exact asset projection omits uploader/storage key/hash. Signed URLs are bearer credentials until expiry: never expose them to the customer or claim immediate server-side revocation. Confirm the deployed contract before depending on it.

Current full-resolution `MediaTransfer.download` requests a live signed URL, uses an ephemeral no-cookie/no-cache session with redirects disabled, checks request generation, size and response, and writes a file-protected temporary file. Existing offline pending captures and saved thumbnails are staff workflows, not a proof of customer visibility or a complete verified customer gallery. Initial handoff therefore requires online validation and live downloads; show a clear retry/connection state, without changing existing offline capture, cash/check, or staff media access.

## Implementation sequence

1. Keep this work isolated from the separately unqualified direct Photos tab. Review current design rules, customer handoff lifecycle and the media transfer scope guards. Add a dedicated customer-facing selection entry to the existing staff job media screen, preserving all current selection and capture controls. Explicitly select only stored, nonarchived photo IDs from the currently validated job page; preview count and require a deliberate handoff. Do not assume all photos are suitable.
2. Introduce a transient handoff owner bound to actor, organization, job, session generation, authority, API request generation, saved-copy scope revision and origin. Use `requireFresh` for the job and each exact-record read (bypassing local saved copies and URL cache; this alone is not proof against intermediary caches) and use the server’s scoped `GET /v1/media/:id` projection for each selected ID (add a matching Swift projection; it omits organization ID because the server scopes lookup to the authenticated organization). Verify exact ID, job, kind, stored and nonarchived status, no duplicates, current actor/organization and token before and after every await. Do not present partial or cached data as a complete selection; report missing/archived/denied records with a safe return to staff. Confirm what the list actually returns on the deployed API; do not infer customer permission from tags.
3. Inside the existing protected customer container, add a minimal read-only gallery of the selected images, with accessible previous/next alternatives to swiping. Hide filename, caption, metadata, stage, staff tools, archive and sharing. Keep hand-back protected. Do not expose signed URLs or raw media records. Paginate or lazily decode the selection, prefetch at most adjacent items, and release images and protected temporary files on exit, background/lock, scope invalidation, failure and cancellation. Recheck token around every download; ignore and delete late completions. Explicitly validate HTTPS target and expiry through the existing transfer contract and do not trust redirect destinations.
4. Tests first: unit tests for selected-ID membership, cross-job/org mismatch, archived/pending/non-photo/duplicate/missing assets, partial/error pages, permission/token changes during reads/downloads, cancellation and temporary-file cleanup; deny stale completions and never widen selected IDs. Exercise connectivity loss before and during download and show retry without reverting to staff cached photos. Mounted phone and narrow iPad tests cover explicit selection, customer-only controls, swipe and previous/next, protected hand-back, revocation/background, accessibility labels and large text. Confirm staff capture/share and offline pending queue remain intact. Independently review exact source and run relevant tests before release.

Unresolved before production: whether the deployed exact-record projection matches this checkout and the bounded number/concurrency of per-asset checks; stale signed URLs remain usable until expiry; and whether the actual deployment matches the source. Do not silently filter inaccessible selections or claim remote sharing, revocation of already viewed bytes, or offline gallery support.
